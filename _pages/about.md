I am a **theoretical physicist** specializing in computational biophysics and statistical mechanics. My work focuses on deriving mechanistic descriptions that connect microscopic interactions to macroscopic dynamics, with a particular interest in living and synthetic biomolecular systems.

Currently, I am a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Doctoral Candidate at **SISSA** (Scuola Internazionale Superiore di Studi Avanzati) within the **BioHYBRITE Doctoral Network**. Working within the Molecular and Statistical Biophysics group under Prof. Cristian Micheletti, my PhD research focuses on the predictive modelling of translocation properties of nucleic acids and protein assemblies. By employing multiscale structural modelling, molecular dynamics, and Monte Carlo simulations, I aim to characterize how biohybrid structures move through nanopores to aid the design of synthetic molecules with tailored mechanical properties.

As an **MSCA Fellow** within the BioHYBRITE network, my research thrives on interdisciplinary and international collaboration[cite: 1]. A key component of my project involves a close collaboration with the **University of Cambridge (UCAM)**, with Prof. Ulrich Keyser serving on my associated advisory team as a secondary supervisor. By integrating with the Keyser lab's experimental activities, we iteratively refine our computational models against single-molecule data to accurately predict structural dynamics. 

Previously, as a **Visiting Student Researcher at TU Berlin**, I worked on non-equilibrium statistical mechanics, utilizing hydrodynamic field theories and coarse-grained Langevin dynamics to understand large-scale structure and transport in active matter systems. 

Beyond my current focus, I maintain a strong background in scientific machine learning. I have previously developed and applied **Physics‑Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)** to solve high-dimensional PDEs and infer effective parameters in fluid dynamics. My broader computational toolkit includes C++, Python, Julia, stochastic simulation, continuum theory, and data-driven inverse methods.

### Research Focus

* **Biomolecular Translocation:** Modelling the response of RNA, DNA, and protein biohybrids to nanopore translocation[cite: 1].
* **Collaborative Biohybrid Design (MSCA):** Working across the European BioHYBRITE network—including an active collaboration with the **University of Cambridge** under the co-advisement of Prof. Ulrich Keyser—to validate coarse-grained models of nucleic acids and protein assemblies[cite: 1].
* **Non-equilibrium Statistical Mechanics:** Analyzing how violations of detailed balance and non-reciprocal couplings at the microscopic scale alter phase behaviour and transport in active matter.
* **Machine Learning for Physics:** Leveraging PINNs and data‑driven methods to solve complex dynamical systems (part of my broader computational toolkit).
